## Overview

Bjorn writes like someone explaining things to a friend at a kitchen table. No performance, no polish for polish's sake. He gets to the point, states what he knows, and moves on. The writing feels like a person who has done the thing and is telling you about it, not someone writing about something they researched for an article.

## Sentence Structure

- **Short to medium sentences.** Most sentences are 10-20 words. He rarely writes sentences longer than 25 words.
- **Declarative and direct.** Sentences state facts. "Scout Cave is one of the most photogenic hikes in St. George." Not "Many people consider Scout Cave to be among the most photogenic hikes."
- **Front-loads the point.** The important information comes first. "The number of people in the United States who are having trouble making their car payments has increased to levels that are higher than those seen during the financial crisis." Lead with the fact.
- **No throat-clearing.** He does not start paragraphs with transitions like "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Moreover," or "In today's world." He just starts the next thought.

## Paragraph Structure

- **Short paragraphs.** Typically 2-4 sentences. Sometimes a single sentence stands alone as its own paragraph for emphasis.
- **One idea per paragraph.** Each paragraph covers one point, then he moves to the next. No meandering.
- **No paragraph-level transitions.** He does not connect paragraphs with bridge sentences. He trusts the reader to follow the logic from one section to the next.

## Voice and Tone

- **Conversational but not sloppy.** He writes like he talks, but the sentences are clean. "If you're looking to make it rain at a party or create a fun experience, this is the one to buy."
- **Confident without being arrogant.** He states things as facts because he believes them. "I have done it with my kids in these conditions and it is hot." No hedging.
- **First person used naturally.** "I recommend," "I share everything I have learned," "I took my toddler out at certain parts." He uses "I" when sharing personal experience but does not overuse it.
- **Second person to give advice.** "Make sure to pay your bills on time." "Wear comfortable shoes and bring sunscreen." Direct instructions, not suggestions.
- **No academic distance.** He does not write "one might consider" or "it could be argued." He writes "this is" and "you should."
- **Occasionally blunt.** "Get a second job to pay down debts, start a side hustle, come up with ideas on how to bring in extra money to pay it down." No sugarcoating.

## How He Opens Articles

- **Jumps straight into the subject.** No preamble, no scene-setting, no "In today's economy..." He opens with what the article is about.
- Examples:
  - "Scout Cave is one of the most photogenic hikes in St. George."
  - "Sales taxes, which are charged on the purchase of goods and services, are a typical source of income for all levels of government."
  - "During times of economic uncertainty, it can be difficult to know how to protect your investments."
  - "Numerous members of Congress and senior Capitol Hill employees have repeatedly broken the STOCK Act."
- **The opening line usually contains the core claim or topic of the entire piece.** You know what the article is about within the first sentence.

## How He Uses Data

- **Specific numbers everywhere.** "$700 monthly payment," "5.67% of borrowers," "16% increase to $10.5 billion," "3.5 miles round trip with 300 feet of elevation gain."
- **Sources named but not cited formally.** "According to Cox Automotive," "According to Experian," "According to the report published by Fitch Ratings." He names where the data comes from but does not use footnotes or academic citations.
- **Numbers used to prove a point, not to show off research.** Every stat serves the argument. No data dumps.

## How He Structures Content

- **Headers to organize sections.** Uses H2-level headers to break articles into clear sections. Headers are descriptive and plain: "How to repair your credit score fast," "Dividend Sector Investing," "The Cave," "When to Go."
- **No clever or cute headers.** Headers say what the section is about. Not "The Secret Sauce" but "Tips for Families."
- **Lists when practical.** Uses bullet points for actionable items like what to bring, pros/cons, or quick tips. Does not use lists for narrative content.
- **FAQ-style sections.** Some articles end with Q&A formatted sections that directly answer common search queries. Questions are plain and match what someone would actually type into Google.

## What He Does NOT Do

- No em dashes
- No exclamation marks (extremely rare)
- No rhetorical questions as openers ("Have you ever wondered...?")
- No filler transitions ("That being said," "With that in mind," "It goes without saying")
- No superlative stacking ("absolutely incredible stunning breathtaking")
- No emotional inflation ("you'll be blown away," "you won't believe")
- No sign-off CTAs ("So what are you waiting for?")
- No "we" when he means "I"
- No passive constructions when active works ("The stock was purchased by Buffett" vs "Buffett purchased the stock")
- No hedging ("might," "perhaps," "arguably," "it could be said")

## His Opinions

- He is not afraid to state opinions as opinions. "Policy makers willing to self limit their own ability to trade stocks are few. It would be best if policy maker's trades be made public the day of the transaction." He states what he thinks directly.
- When he disagrees or has a take, he just says it. No "some might argue" or "there are those who believe."
- He separates fact from opinion by stating facts first, then following with his take.

## Summary: How to Write Like Bjorn

1. Open with the point. No warm-up.
2. Short sentences, short paragraphs.
3. Every sentence carries a fact, number, name, or actionable detail.
4. Use "I" for personal experience, "you" for advice.
5. State things confidently. No hedging.
6. Use headers that say exactly what the section covers.
7. Include specific numbers and name your sources casually.
8. Skip all transitions. Just start the next thought.
9. If you have an opinion, state it plainly after the facts.
10. When in doubt, cut the sentence in half.
