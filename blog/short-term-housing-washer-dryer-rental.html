Temporary Housing in St. George Lacks Laundry

Furnished rentals, corporate housing, and extended-stay units in St. George typically come with a bed, a couch, and a kitchen. Laundry hookups are there. The machines are not. Landlords leave the laundry closet empty because tenants cycle in and out every few months.

This puts you in a bad spot. You have hookups and no machines. You are here for 3 to 6 months. You need to figure out laundry on day one.

The standard options are buying appliances, using a laundromat, or renting machines. Two of those options cost too much time or money for a temporary stay.

Buying Appliances for a Short Stay Is a Bad Deal

A basic washer and dryer set costs $800 to $1,200. A mid-range pair runs about $1,500. You are spending that money for machines you will use 3 to 6 months, then need to sell, store, or abandon.

Selling used appliances in a city you are leaving is stressful. You need to find a buyer, negotiate a price, and coordinate the pickup before your move-out date. Used washers and dryers sell for 40 to 60 percent of what you paid. A $1,500 set sells for $600 to $900 if you are lucky.

That means you lose $600 to $900 on appliances you used for a few months. The math does not work for temporary housing.

Laundromats Cost More Than You Think

A single load at a St. George laundromat costs $3 to $5 for washing and $2 to $4 for drying. A two-person household does 4 to 6 loads per week. That is $20 to $54 per week in machine costs alone.

The bigger cost is time. Each laundromat trip takes 90 minutes to 2 hours. Travel nurses working 12-hour shifts at St. George Regional do not have 2 hours to sit at a laundromat on their day off. Construction workers putting in 10-hour days on Washington County job sites have the same problem.

Over a 4-month stay, laundromat costs add up to $320 to $864 in quarters. You also lose 30 to 40 hours sitting in a laundromat. That time has value, especially when your schedule is already packed.
