How Much Does It Cost to Rent a Washer and Dryer in 2026?

Published September 17, 2026

Renting a washer and dryer costs $30 to $110 per month in 2026. Most renters pay $50 to $75 for a standard set with delivery, installation, and repairs included. Rent-to-own stores charge the most at $85 to $150 per month.

The price depends on who you rent from, what kind of machines you get, and how long you commit. Here is what each option costs per month and per year, what the upfront fees look like, and when renting beats buying or the laundromat.

Washer and Dryer Rental Cost Per Month

Local rental companies are the cheapest. Most charge $30 to $65 per month for a washer and dryer set. The lowest advertised prices sit around $30 and usually require a 12-month contract. Month-to-month rentals run $50 to $65.

National rental services charge $60 to $110 for a standard set. Premium front-load sets run $90 to $140. Stackable units run $100 to $200.

Apartment complexes rent in-unit machines for $35 to $125 per month. Most land between $40 and $75. The fee gets added to your rent and you cannot shop around. Ask what the fee is before you sign the lease. If the unit has washer and dryer hookups, you can usually rent from an outside company for less.

A single machine costs more than half the price of a set. Renting the pair is the better deal.

Stackable units cost the most because rental companies stock fewer of them. Measure your laundry space before you order. A side-by-side set needs about 56 inches of width. A stacked unit needs about 28 inches of width and 80 inches of height.

Upfront Fees: Delivery, Installation, and Deposits

The monthly price is not the only cost. Most companies charge something on day one.

  • Delivery and installation: $0 to $100. Some companies include it. Others charge a flat fee.
  • Security deposit: $0 to $100. Deposits are more common with national services and 12-month contracts.
  • Credit check: most local companies skip it. Rent-to-own stores skip it too and charge higher prices.
  • Early cancellation: one to two months of rent on a fixed-term contract. Month-to-month rentals have no cancellation fee.

Rent-to-Own Is Not the Same as Renting

Rent-to-own stores like Rent-A-Center and Aaron's price by the week. A washer and dryer set runs $20 to $35 per week. That is $85 to $150 per month. You own the machines after 12 to 24 months of payments.

The total is the problem. A mid-range set at $30 per week costs $2,340 over 78 weeks. The same set sells for $900 to $1,000 at a big-box store. You pay 2 to 3 times the retail price.

Rent-to-own makes sense in one case. Most stores offer a 90-day same-as-cash window. Pay the machines off inside 90 days and you pay close to retail. Past that window, every payment adds to the markup.

A flat rental works differently. You never own the machines. You pay a lower monthly price, repairs are included, and you cancel when you no longer need them.

What Is Included in a Washer and Dryer Rental

A standard rental covers:

  • Delivery and installation
  • Connection to your existing water, drain, power, and dryer vent
  • Repairs for normal wear and tear
  • A replacement machine if yours cannot be fixed
  • Pickup when you cancel

It does not cover water and electricity. Those run $1.00 to $2.00 per load. Damage from misuse is also on you.

Rental machines are usually not new. Expect a cleaned and tested Whirlpool, Maytag, or GE that is a few years old. That matters less when the rental company pays for every repair.

Renting vs. the Laundromat

The average laundromat costs $4.00 to $8.00 per load. A family of 3 to 4 spends $100 to $150 per month. With a $60 rental, the same laundry costs $85 to $110 including water and electricity.

The bigger savings is time. Two laundromat trips a week take 8 to 16 hours a month. With machines at home, a load takes 2 minutes to start.

A single person doing 6 loads a month spends $40 to $65 at the laundromat. A rental costs about the same at that volume. It wins on time and convenience, not price.

Renting vs. Buying

A mid-range washer and dryer set costs about $1,500 all-in with delivery, installation, and tax. At $60 a month, renting reaches that number at month 25.

Renting costs less if you stay under 2.5 years. Buying costs less if you stay longer and the machines do not break. One $300 repair pushes the break-even point to 30 months. Here is the full rent vs. buy comparison and the hidden costs of owning.

Renters have one more reason to rent. Machines you own have to move with you, and the next place may have different hookups or machines already installed.

What to Check Before You Sign

  • Contract length. Month-to-month costs a little more and lets you leave anytime.
  • Cancellation terms. Ask how much notice you need to give and whether pickup is free.
  • Repair response time. The standard is 24 to 48 hours. A week without a washer defeats the purpose.
  • Your hookups. Confirm electric or gas, and vented or ventless, before delivery day.
  • Who pays for what. Normal wear is the company's problem. Misuse is yours.
  • Price increases. Ask if the monthly rate is locked.
